# Getting started with your Pi — a plain-English owner's guide

This guide is for anyone who has received one of our small Pi boxes and is not a
developer. You do not need any technical knowledge. Follow the steps in order and
you will be up and running in about five minutes.

**Need help at any point?** QCAI Support — **1-855-782-6824** —
**support@quantumconnectai.com**

## 1. What's in the box

Your box should contain:

- **The Pi itself** — a small computer, about the size of a deck of cards. It is
  already set up for you. There is nothing to install.
- **A power cable** — this is the only cable you must connect.
- **A memory card** — a tiny card already inside the Pi. It holds everything the Pi
  needs. Please leave it where it is.
- **Possibly an antenna or a network cable**, depending on how your unit connects
  to the internet.

If anything looks damaged or is missing, call QCAI Support on 1-855-782-6824
before going further.

## 2. Plugging it in

1. Place the Pi somewhere dry, with a little space around it so air can move.
2. If you plan to use a network cable (see section 3), plug it in **before** the
   power.
3. Plug the power cable into the Pi, then into the wall.
4. Wait about **two minutes** the first time. The Pi is waking up and getting
   itself ready.

**What the little lights mean:**

- **Red light on** — the Pi has power. This is good.
- **Green light blinking** — the Pi is thinking and working. This is also good.
- **No lights at all** — see "If something seems wrong" in section 6.

## 3. Getting it online — pick one

Your Pi needs the internet to do its job. There are three ways, and you only need
one of them. Pick whichever is easiest for you.

### Option A — network cable (easiest)

If your office internet box (router) is nearby, this is the simplest choice.

1. Plug one end of the network cable into the Pi.
2. Plug the other end into a spare socket on your router.
3. Plug in the Pi's power.

That is all. There is nothing to type and nothing to press. Skip to section 4.

### Option B — Wi-Fi

1. Power on the Pi and wait two minutes.
2. On your phone or laptop, open the list of Wi-Fi networks, just like joining
   Wi-Fi anywhere else.
3. Join the network called **QConnect-Setup**. (If it does not appear after two
   minutes, unplug the power, wait ten seconds, and plug it back in.)
4. A setup page opens by itself. It says **QConnect Setup** at the top. If it does
   not open on its own, open your web browser and type **10.0.0.1** into the
   address bar.
5. On that page, choose your normal Wi-Fi network from the list and type its
   password, exactly as it appears on your router sticker.
6. Tap **Connect**. The Pi will now join your Wi-Fi and the QConnect-Setup network
   will disappear.

If you typed the password wrong, nothing breaks — the setup network comes back
and you can try again.

### Option C — cell phone SIM (AT&T)

If your Pi came with a SIM card (a phone-style internet connection), it is already
set up. You do not need to type anything.

1. Place the Pi somewhere with a decent phone signal — near a window helps.
2. Plug in the power and wait up to five minutes. Cell connections take a little
   longer to say hello the first time.

## 4. How to know it worked

You do not need to check anything technical. Here is the simple test:

- If you used **Wi-Fi**, the setup page will say **Connected** after step 6 above.
- Whatever option you used, the Pi checks in with our fleet dashboard by itself
  within a few minutes. The person who shipped your box can see it appear there,
  and that is how we both know everything is fine.

If the Pi shows up on the dashboard, you are done. Leave it plugged in.

## 5. Everyday use

There is nothing to do day to day.

- Leave the Pi plugged in and powered on.
- It reports in, and updates itself, automatically.
- There are no buttons to press and no screens to check.

The best thing you can do for your Pi is simply not disturb it.

## 6. If something seems wrong

Find the symptom below and try the steps in order. Nothing you try here can hurt
the Pi.

| What you see | What it usually means | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| No lights at all | No power | Check the plug is fully in at both ends; try a different wall socket. |
| Red light only, green never blinks | The memory card may be loose | Unplug the power, gently push the tiny card fully into its slot, plug back in. |
| The QConnect-Setup network never appears | Setup needs another try | Unplug power, wait ten seconds, plug back in, wait two full minutes. |
| The setup page won't open | The page didn't pop up by itself | While connected to QConnect-Setup, open a browser and type 10.0.0.1. |
| It worked before but is now offline | Wi-Fi password changed, or it lost signal | Did the office Wi-Fi password change? If so, rejoin QConnect-Setup and enter the new one. For SIM units, move the Pi near a window. |

**When to call us:** if you have tried the steps above and it still isn't working,
call QCAI Support on **1-855-782-6824** or write to
**support@quantumconnectai.com**. When you call or write, say:

> "The box labelled device **[the name on the sticker]** is showing
> **[what you see — for example, 'no lights' or 'offline']**, and I've tried
> **[what you tried]**."

That one sentence tells us almost everything we need.

## 7. The one rule

**Never pull the memory card out while the Pi is powered on, and never wipe,
erase or rewrite the card** — even if another device asks you to. The card is the
Pi's brain. If it is removed while running, or erased, the Pi will stop working
and will need to come back to us.

If you need to unplug the Pi, just pull the power plug. That is always safe.

## 8. Returning or moving your Pi

**Moving it to a new spot:** unplug the power, move it, plug it back in. If it
uses Wi-Fi and you moved beyond your Wi-Fi's reach, set it up again with the
steps in Option B.

**Sending it back:** unplug the power, pack the Pi and its cables, and tell the
person who shipped it that it is on its way back — this stops our dashboard from
raising a "missing device" alert while it travels.
